# Research: Evidence-based team and release-engineering practices (facet 1 of 3)

## Highest-confidence practices (ranked by strength of evidence)

1. **Code review mechanics.** Google (Sadowski et al., ICSE-SEIP 2018, internal data): median change = **24 lines modified**; >35% touch one file, ~90% <10 files; **median 1 reviewer**, <25% have >1; median end-to-end review latency **<4 h**; devs spend median **2.6 h/week** reviewing. <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3183519.3183525> · <https://sback.it/publications/icse2018seip.pdf> Microsoft (Bacchelli & Bird, ICSE 2013; 570 comments/200 threads): "defect" is only the **4th** most frequent comment category — 78 comments (**14%**), mostly low-level — behind code improvements (165; 29%); realized benefits are knowledge transfer and awareness. <https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ICSE202013-codereview.pdf> McIntosh et al. (MSR 2014, Qt/VTK/ITK): coverage negatively associated with post-release defects in all models but significant in only 2 of 4 releases; components with lax participation carry an estimated **up to five additional post-release defects** (self-approval coef. 1.34, p<0.001). <https://posl.ait.kyushu-u.ac.jp/~kamei/publications/McIntosh_MSR2014.pdf> **Confidence: high** for descriptive figures (direct measurement); **medium** for causality.

2. **Small batches / short-lived branches / trunk-based development.** DORA defines it as <3 active branches, merges within ~a day, no code-freeze periods <https://dora.dev/capabilities/trunk-based-development/>. In DORA's 2022 structural-equation model, lift to organizational performance from trunk-based development was **36% → 1525%** (below- vs above-average documentation quality); CI **34% → 750%**; continuous delivery **63% → 656%**; loosely coupled teams **46% → 313%**; SRE **79% → 343%**. <https://dora.dev/capabilities/documentation-quality/> **Confidence: medium-high** — very large samples, but cross-sectional/SEM (association, not proven causation).

3. **Deployment automation, test automation, CI, continuous delivery.** DORA reports CD improves delivery performance and availability, reduces rework/unplanned work, "predicts lower levels of burnout"; elite teams meeting reliability targets are **3× more likely** to have loosely coupled architecture. <https://dora.dev/capabilities/continuous-delivery/> **Confidence: medium-high** (same methodology).

4. **Peer review instead of change-approval boards.** DORA's 2019 research: CAB/external approval "have a negative impact on software delivery performance", and "no evidence was found to support the hypothesis that a more formal, external review process was associated with lower change fail rates"; heavyweight approval drives larger, less frequent batches, raising risk; "responding to problems by adding more process ... will make things worse". <https://dora.dev/capabilities/streamlining-change-approval/> **Confidence: high as a stated research finding**, medium on mechanism.

5. **Mutation testing as a test-quality signal.** Just et al. (FSE 2014): 357 real faults, 5 Java programs, 230k mutants; mutant detection correlates positively with real-fault detection independently of coverage (Â12 0.66–0.81; ≥0.71 = "large"); mutants coupled to **73%** of real faults. <https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rjust/publ/mutants_real_faults_fse_2014.pdf> **Confidence: high** (controlled experiment) — but see the contradiction below.

6. **Shift-left security.** DORA: high performers "spend 50 percent less time remediating security issues" (2016 data). <https://dora.dev/capabilities/pervasive-security/> **Confidence: medium** (old, survey-based).

7. **Documentation quality.** DORA 2023: generative culture ↔ **30% higher** org performance; "high-quality documentation drastically increases the effectiveness of technical capabilities". <https://dora.dev/research/2023/dora-report/> **Confidence: medium-high** (self-report survey).

8. **AI-assisted development — freshest, most contested.** DORA 2024: >75% of respondents use AI daily; >⅓ report moderate-to-extreme productivity gains; per **25% increase in AI adoption**: +7.5% documentation quality, +3.4% code quality, +3.1% review speed, but **−1.5% delivery throughput and −7.2% delivery stability**; **39%** report little/no trust. <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/devops-sre/announcing-the-2024-dora-report> · <https://dora.dev/ai/gen-ai-report/> DORA 2025 (nearly 5,000 respondents): AI is "an amplifier"; adoption now correlates **positively** with throughput and product performance but still **negatively with stability**; 90% use AI at work, >80% report gains, 30% low trust; gains require loosely coupled architecture, fast feedback loops, "strong automated testing". <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report> **Confidence: medium.**

## Contested or ineffective

- **RCTs disagree on AI productivity.** Google-internal RCT: ~**21% less time** on a complex task, wide confidence interval <https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12944v2>. METR RCT: 16 experienced OSS devs, 246 real tasks — **19% longer**, while those devs predicted ~20% speedup <https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/> · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089>.
- **Code coverage as a gate.** Inozemtseva & Holmes (ICSE 2014, Distinguished Paper): coverage/effectiveness correlation drops to low–moderate once suite size is controlled; "high coverage does not indicate that a test suite is effective". <https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2568225.2568271> · <https://blog.acolyer.org/2014/10/21/coverage-is-not-strongly-correlated-with-test-suite-effectiveness/>
- **Mutation score as a metric.** Papadakis et al. 2018 (CoREBench + Defects4J): with suite size controlled, correlations fall to **~0.05–0.20** and mutants are "relatively unreliable substitutes"; yet top-ranked suites detect **8–11%** (Defects4J) and **18–46%** (CoreBench) more faults — useful as *guidance*, poor as an effectiveness metric. <https://coinse.github.io/publications/pdfs/Papadakis2018hi.pdf>
- **Test pyramid vs trophy.** No empirical validation of either shape found. Closest: Trautsch et al. (JSS 2020) — "neither unit nor integration tests are better in detecting certain defect types"; existing definitions "do not fit modern" contexts <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0164121219301955>. Contract testing: case-study evidence only (CDCT caught every defect across 23 interactions in one thesis <https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/e035e9e7-b7a8-43c2-8c37-8020ae36dfee/content>); the repeated "CDCT reduces integration issues by 60% (Pinheiro & Simão 2019)" has **no traceable primary source**.
- **Progressive delivery (canary/blue-green/flags).** Described as practice by Google SRE <https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/>, but no peer-reviewed measurement of reduced change-failure rate was found. Feature-flag benefits are researcher inference, not measured results.
- **Postmortems / blameless culture.** Google's chapter is first-party practice, not a study <https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/>. The common figures "20–35% fewer repeat incident classes" and "resolve repeat failures 2.5× faster" trace only to vendor marketing (BigPanda, itoc360) — **no primary source; do not quote.**
- **SLOs / error budgets.** First-party Google workbook guidance <https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/>; no measured-impact study located.
- **Design docs / RFCs / ADRs.** Only first-party practice descriptions (Google, Microsoft, AWS) plus a 2026 empirical comparison of ADR *templates* <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27333v1> — **no evidence that written design artifacts improve delivery or defect outcomes.** Folklore.
- **DORA-metric targets.** The 2025 report says metrics "tell you what is happening but not why", replacing single-metric comparison with seven team archetypes <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report/>.
- **"Trunk-based development causes burnout."** Claimed in a Medium post citing DORA 2023; DORA's 2023 key-findings page does not say this <https://dora.dev/research/2023/dora-report/>. Unverified.
- **AI-generated code security.** Veracode (vendor; 80 tasks, 4 languages, 4 CWE classes, 100+ LLMs): only **55%** of tasks produced secure code (45% had a known flaw; Java pass rate 29%); the Oct-2025 update shows ~50–59% for non-OpenAI models, GPT-5 70% <https://www.veracode.com/blog/ai-generated-code-security-risks/> · <https://www.veracode.com/wp-content/uploads/October-2025-GenAI-Code-Security-Report-Update.pdf>. Caveat: SAST-only measurement, which a critic argues is a flawed instrument (David Brumley, surfaced via source_check).
- **AI code-quality drift.** GitClear (vendor; 211M changed lines, 2020–2024): refactored lines fell 25% → <10%; copy/pasted (cloned) lines rose 8.3% → 12.3%. Correlational. <https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research>
- **Trunk vs git-flow.** A 2025 survey/interview study reported power = 32.9% and explicitly declined to draw conclusions <https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08943v1>. No strong evidence either way.

## Uncertainty / what I could not verify

- **No AI-era evidence for most practices.** Nearly all strong numbers predate 2023 (Google 2018, Microsoft 2013, McIntosh 2014, mutation 2014/2018). Post-2023 quantitative evidence found is limited to DORA 2024/2025, the two AI RCTs, Veracode and GitClear — self-report surveys, small RCTs, or vendor research.
- **DORA is not causal.** DORA frames findings as hypotheses to replicate locally <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/devops-sre/announcing-the-2024-dora-report>. The 2024 full-report PDF (20 MB+) was unretrievable, so 2024 figures come from Google Cloud/DORA first-party summaries; no independent replication located.
- **PDF extraction limitation.** Google/Microsoft/McIntosh/mutation numbers were extracted from PDFs via an automated answer-extraction path, not manually verified character-by-character against facsimiles.
- **Unexamined gaps:** flag/progressive-delivery failure modes, rollback vs roll-forward practice, definition-of-done artifacts, and independent verification of the postmortem/SLO claims.
